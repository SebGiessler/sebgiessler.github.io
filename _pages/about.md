I'm a philosopher of science and technology and  I work on research questions at the intersection between Philosophy of Science and the social sciences, namely cultural anthropology and sociology. On the one hand, I focus on the integration of ethics and epistemology of science to analyse the connection between computer-assisted scientific tools and the normative and ethical implications that these tools entail. On the other hand, I typically peruse methods from qualitative social research and Science and Technology Studies. I integrate empirical approaches into my research to look into epistemic practices in technology development and use these findings to formulate which epistemic and non-epistemic values influence the integration of ethical and societal aspects into technology development.

This range of research interests, combined with my experience in policy research and field research, has come together to form a personal project that can be described by the working title ‘Ethics and Epistemology of Computation for Science’. It's aim is to integrate perspectives of ethics and epistemology of science, to examine how computation, i.e. machine learning tools, novel methods of data analysis or synthetic data, shape scientific practices and to pose the question, what theoretical, methodological and ethical implications this entails.

I want to investigate whether, and if so how, scientific practices from computer science or engineering have an impact when these methods are applied in other contexts or these contexts shift. Examples for this are methodology shifts in social research (i.e. using Chatbots as surrogate for interviewers, in-silico sampling, synthetic data), ethical issues of internet measurement in Computer Science or new analytical methods and machine learning tools in big science. What these diverse cases have in common is that computation is transforming established scientific practices, putting them under pressure, or replacing them entirely with alternative practices that may be based on different epistemological foundations. 

Current research
======
At the International Center for Ethics in the Sciences and Humanities (IZEW), I worked as research associate in the project Ethical and Societal Aspects of Autonomous Robotics (EGAR), a research project associated with seven robotics research and development projects. These projects span the entire stack of robotics, from hardware and software development to operational deployment. These projects cover a wide range of topics in current robotics research, such as sensors and computer vision, autonomous drones, medical technology, and agricultural robots. My tasks there included analysing the ethical, social and legal aspects of autonomous robotics from a technology assessment perspective. Furthermore, I analysed the development of mitigation strategies tailored to specific projects and their respective issues along the research areas of autonomy and responsibility, fairness and transparency, privacy, and informational self-determination. As a philosopher embedded in robotics labs, I conducted qualitative field research in the associated projects and offered guidance on dealing with informal and regulatory requirements in technology development.
